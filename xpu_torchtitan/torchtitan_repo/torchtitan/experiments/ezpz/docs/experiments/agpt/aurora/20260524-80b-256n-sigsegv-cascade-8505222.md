# 80B 256N Production — SIGSEGV Cascade, Wrapper Exhausted (job 8505222)

> **2026-05-24, job `8505222`** — 80B 256N production dispatch via
> the failover wrapper failed deterministically: every attempt died in
> init with SIGSEGV (signal 11) on a different node, wrapper swapped
> spare in, retry same failure, exhausted 5 retries (6 attempts total).
> **Zero checkpoints persisted.** This is a *different* failure mode
> from the [blendcorpus EOFError
> race](../../../guides/known-bugs/blendcorpus-eoferror-race.md) seen
> at 80B 8N, and a *different* failure mode from the [silent hang the
> wrapper recovers
> from](20260523-failover-silent-hang-recovery-8505298.md).

## TL;DR

| Attempt | Bad node identified | Bad rank | Signal | Wrapper action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | x4013c2s3b0n0 (rank 1612) | 1612 | 15 | swap → x4101c5s5b0n0 |
| 2 | x4101c5s5b0n0 (ranks 1, 9) | 1, 9 | 11, 15 | swap → x4101c5s6b0n0 |
| 3 | x4101c5s6b0n0 (ranks 9, 1) | 9, 1 | 11, 15 | swap → x4101c6s1b0n0 |
| 4 | x4101c6s1b0n0 (ranks 1, 11) | 1, 11 | 15, 11 | swap → x4015c2s4b0n0 |
| 5 | x4015c2s4b0n0 (rank 698) | 698 | 11 | swap → x4100c7s6b0n0 |
| 6 | x4100c7s6b0n0 (rank 2535) | 2535 | 15 | **wrapper bails** |

Every swapped-in spare was also a SIGSEGV machine. Wrapper was working
correctly — it identified the bad node from the log, swapped in a
spare from the available pool, retried. **The Aurora bad-node rate at
80B init in this 256N dispatch was too high for the available spare
count.**

## Anatomy of one attempt (representative)

Attempts ran 97-102 seconds each — mid-init, before training started.
Each attempt:
1. Yeet completes, mpiexec launches 3072 ranks (256 nodes × 12 GPUs)
2. Model construction begins
3. One rank (typically the lowest rank on the bad node — rank 1 means
   GPU 1 of node-0-spare) hits SIGSEGV mid-`init_process_group` or
   mid-`set_determinism`
4. Sibling ranks on the same node get SIGTERM (signal 15) cascade
5. mpiexec reports exit 143 (128+15) after ~95s
6. Wrapper sees the "rank X died from signal Y" pattern, scrapes the
   hostname, marks the node bad, swaps in next spare from the pool,
   restarts

## Spatial clustering of bad nodes

Of the 6 bad nodes identified, **3 came from the
`x4101c5b0` rack neighborhood**:
- `x4101c5s5b0n0` (attempt 2)
- `x4101c5s6b0n0` (attempt 3)
- `x4101c6s1b0n0` (attempt 4)

The wrapper's blind-swap picks spares from the available spare pool
serially. The spare pool for 8505222 (selected at PBS scheduling
time) appears to have over-sampled this rack neighborhood. **This
is not visible to the wrapper** — it just sees "next spare available"
and uses it. Spatially-aware spare allocation (avoid clustering
spares in the same rack/router as known-bad nodes) could help, but
that's an Aurora-scheduler-level concern.

The other 3 bad nodes (`x4013c2s3b0n0`, `x4015c2s4b0n0`,
`x4016c5s0b0n0`, `x4100c5s7b0n0`, `x4100c7s6b0n0`) are scattered
across different rack chassis with no obvious pattern.

## Why this is hard to retry around

The 80B 256N config requests **256 train + 20 spare = 276 nodes**
(`-l select=276`). PBS allocated 276 nodes from its current pool;
the wrapper consumed 6 of the 20 spares (5 swaps + 1 final retry that
also failed) before bailing. **At a ~2-3% Aurora SIGSEGV rate on this
config**, you'd need ~7-15 spares to expect a non-bad swap pool, and
this 20-spare allocation happened to draw 6 bad ones in sequence.

Possible mitigations (in order of cost):
1. **Bump `-l select=` to 296** (40 spares instead of 20). Doubles the
   spare overhead but means even at a 5% bad-node rate the wrapper
   has working spares to find.
2. **Retry the dispatch instead of bailing** when wrapper exhausts —
   i.e. on bail, requeue the whole job with afterany. The new PBS
   allocation draws fresh nodes from a different rotation.
3. **File ALCF ticket** about the bad-node clustering pattern. This
   may be a known/seasonal hardware issue.

## What this is NOT

- **Not the blendcorpus EOFError race.** That's a data-pipeline bug
  reproduced at 80B 8N (see
  [`blendcorpus-eoferror-race.md`](../../../guides/known-bugs/blendcorpus-eoferror-race.md)).
  8505222 logs show ZERO EOFError lines. The 80B 256N dispatch never
  got past init, so the cache-build race didn't have a chance to fire.
- **Not the silent-hang pattern** the wrapper handles cleanly (see
  [`20260523-failover-silent-hang-recovery-8505298.md`](20260523-failover-silent-hang-recovery-8505298.md)).
  SIGSEGV produces explicit crash patterns and explicit exit codes;
  wrapper classifies correctly. The problem isn't classification, it's
  spare-pool quality.
- **Not the async-save cluster cascade** seen at 2B/20B 512N. 8505222
  never reached a step boundary, let alone a checkpoint save.

## Status

Open. 80B production has been stuck on this exact pattern for **11+
dispatches since 2026-05-11**. None of the 80B dispatches has ever
persisted a checkpoint at production scale. The only successful 80B
training to date is the **2026-05-05 smoke at 4N (job 12466025)**
which trained 20 steps cleanly (see
[`20260418-80b-tp2-restored.md`](20260418-80b-tp2-restored.md)).

**Next steps for 80B production** (in priority order):
1. Try bumping `-l select=296` for next dispatch (40 spares vs 20).
2. If still failing, file an ALCF ticket with the bad-node host list
   and request a scheduling change that avoids the `x4101c5/c6`
   neighborhood.
3. Consider a **128N or 64N dispatch** as a middle ground — smaller
   spare-pool draw, may avoid the high-bad-rate rack groups.
