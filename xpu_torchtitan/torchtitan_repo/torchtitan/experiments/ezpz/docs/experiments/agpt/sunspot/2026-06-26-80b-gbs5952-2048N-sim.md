# 80B GBS=5952 2048N-batch simulation (Sunspot, 2026-06-26)

Fourth and largest rung of the 80B global-batch simulation campaign:
GAS=32 at fixed 62N (dp_degree=186) -> **GBS=5952**, simulating the
global batch an Aurora **2048N** run would see (16x the validated
GBS=372), inside the NaN-free `dp_degree <= 186` corner.

**Result: NaN at step 29.** Unlike the 4x (GBS=1488) and 8x (GBS=2976)
rungs, which held NaN-free, GBS=5952 ran 28 clean steps then
`grad_norm = nan` at step 29 (loss still finite that step -- the
documented grad-path-first signature). **This is the first batch-scaling
rung to break.**

See [`2026-06-25-80b-gbs1488-512N-sim.md`](2026-06-25-80b-gbs1488-512N-sim.md)
for the campaign method (why GAS not nodes) and the dp_degree<=186
derivation, shared with this run.

## Config

- Job `12469627`, Sunspot `workq`, 62 active + 4 spare (select=66),
  `scripts/submit_agpt_80b_autoretry.sh`.
- `agpt_80b`, TP=4, LBS=1, **GAS=32 -> GBS=5952**, dp_degree=186,
  AdamW **LR=1e-6**, bf16-compute / fp32-master, AC=full, compile=OFF,
  `VALIDATOR_ENABLE=0`.
- `--np=744`, ~1220 s/step, 10h walltime cap.
- The blendcorpus index cold-built at 744 ranks on attempt 1 and hit the
  build-then-load race (`EOFError`) at this larger index size;
  **auto-retry self-healed** (attempt 2 loaded the now-warm cache) at the
  cost of one spare. Training itself ran on attempt 2.

## The NaN is batch-size-dependent at matched step + effective LR

The lower-GBS rungs ran *past* step 29 and were clean there -- this is
not a step-count artifact:

| GBS | Simulates | step 28 grad_norm | **step 29 grad_norm** | ran to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1488 | 512N | 14.32 | **10.38** (clean) | step 46 |
| 2976 | 1024N | 14.17 | **9.75** (clean) | step 34 |
| 5952 | **2048N** | 15.35 | **nan** | died step 29 |

At the *identical* step 29, 1488 and 2976 recovered (grad_norm ~10,
descending) while 5952 went NaN. All three rode the same step-22-27
elevated-grad transient (peaks ~22-23); only the 16x batch failed to come
down off it.

## CRITICAL caveat: LR was not batch-scaled, and the run was inside warmup

This result must be read with two confounds stated plainly:

1. **LR was flat at 1e-6 for every rung** (372 -> 5952), with NO
   batch-size scaling. The textbook large-batch rule would *raise* LR for
   a bigger batch; here all batches used the same LR. So GBS=5952 was, if
   anything, *under*-LR'd for its batch -- the opposite of the usual
   too-high-LR NaN cause.
2. **The run never left warmup.** The config's `warmup_steps=200` was
   auto-clamped to `total_steps=50` (`components/lr_scheduler:108`), so
   effective LR rose linearly and was only **~5.8e-7 at the step-29 NaN**
   (29/50 of the nominal 1e-6) -- still sub-nominal and increasing.

So the precise, defensible statement is: **at matched effective-LR
(~5.8e-7) and matched step count, the GAS=32-accumulated batch reaches a
grad-path NaN where GAS=8/16 do not.** This is consistent with the
documented grad_norm-NaN-first failure being in the gradient
accumulation / all-reduce path (a larger GAS sum overflowing something),
independent of -- and prior to -- the optimizer's (tiny) LR step.

What this does **NOT** establish: that GBS=5952 NaNs at the *production*
LR=1e-6 (it never reached it), nor that proper batch-scaled LR would help
or hurt. Two follow-up jobs probed exactly this:

- **12469698 (DONE): GBS=5952, LR=1.6e-5 (16x linear-scaled),
  warmup=5 -> NaN at step 7** (loss 11.58 @ step 5 -> 20.38 @ step 6,
  grad_norm 121.9 -> NaN @ step 7). Scaling LR *up* for the larger batch
  made it **far worse** (step 7 vs step 29). 1.6e-5 sits at the LR-finder
  divergence shoulder, so this is an LR-too-high blowup, distinct from the
  flat-LR step-29 batch effect. **Conclusion: do NOT linearly scale LR
  with batch here -- the LR ceiling is fixed by the bf16/dim-9216
  overflow, not the batch.** See the synthesis report below.
- **12469699 (queued): GBS=5952, LR=1e-6, warmup=200, 60 steps** --
  reproducibility of the flat-LR step-29 NaN (deterministic vs
  XPU-execution-order nondeterminism). Pending.

**The full LR / batch / dp-degree story** (LR-finder optimal LR, the
scaled-LR NaN, and the dp-degree bisect showing the corner survives past
dp=186) is consolidated in
[`2026-06-26-80b-lr-batch-dpdegree-findings.md`](2026-06-26-80b-lr-batch-dpdegree-findings.md).

## Campaign summary

| Job | GBS | Simulates | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12469551 | 372 | native | clean, 12.93 -> 7.72 @ 100, 0 NaN |
| 12469609 | 1488 | 512N | clean, -> 8.84 @ 46, 0 NaN |
| 12469626 | 2976 | 1024N | clean, -> 9.84 @ 34, 0 NaN |
| 12469627 | 5952 | **2048N** | **NaN @ step 29** |

Corrected headline: the TP=4/LBS=1/bf16 corner (at flat LR=1e-6, inside
warmup) holds NaN-free to **8x** the validated batch (GBS=2976) but
breaks at **16x** (GBS=5952). The 16x failure is batch-dependent at
matched step+effective-LR, but its dependence on the full nominal /
batch-scaled LR is not yet known (see the two follow-up jobs).

These batch sims still do **not** establish anything about
`dp_degree > 186` (the actual >512N grad regime) or the >512N init path
-- those need the node-count cliff bisect (queued).
