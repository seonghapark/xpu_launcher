# TPC26 MAPE Talk — Working Outline (2026-05-21)

Working notes for the invited talk at the TPC26 MAPE track (Baltimore /
Munich, May 31 – June 3, 2026). Organizer: Rio Yokota (Inst. of Science
Tokyo / RIKEN). Audience: full-training-lifecycle engineers on massive
distributed systems — data curation, cluster stability, node-failure
management, infrastructure pain.

## Title

**Production Pre-Training at Scale: The Good, the Bad, and the Restarts —
Lessons from AuroraGPT**

## Abstract (current draft)

Production pre-training at scale is less a story of peak FLOP/s than of
what works, what breaks, scaling and tuning hyper-parameters and how
cheaply you recover: corrupted shards, ECC errors, fabric flaps, silent
data corruption, and the operational cost of separating transient from
systemic faults. This talk describes the open-source stack we have built
and battle-tested through the AuroraGPT effort at the Argonne Leadership
Computing Facility, where we routinely train across thousands of Intel
GPU nodes on Aurora. The stack pairs `blendcorpus` for reproducible,
weighted data blending over petabyte-scale scientific corpora with a fork
of `torchtitan` extended to target Intel XPUs and the SYCL toolchain,
driven by `ezpz`: our orchestration layer that makes distributed PyTorch
launches portable across {NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, MPS, CPU} with zero code
changes. We will walk through concrete pain points at each layer,
including upstream data churn and shard reproducibility, collective
tuning and dataloader behavior on non-NVIDIA accelerators, and
node-failure management at scale. We will describe our efforts with
various first and second order optimizers and related tuning for scaling
pre-training. We will close on the good, the bad, and the restarts: what
generalizes across vendors, what we got wrong, and the open questions we
would like the TPC community to help us answer.

## Outline

Sections sized at ~5 min each → ~50 min talk + Q&A. Trim by collapsing
§2+§3 or §7+§8 if the slot is shorter.

### 1. The stack in one slide

- AuroraGPT setting: Aurora (Intel Max 1550 / SYCL / xccl), Sunspot
  staging, Polaris (NVIDIA), dev on MPS/CPU
- Three layers: **blendcorpus** (data) → **torchtitan-fork** (model +
  parallelism) → **ezpz** (launcher / env / distributed config)
- One claim: same binary, zero code changes, four vendor backends —
  that claim is the talk's spine

### 2. blendcorpus — the boring layer that breaks first

- Weighted multi-corpus blending over petabyte-scale scientific data
- Reproducibility surface: deterministic shard indexing, weight-stable
  resumption
- Pain: upstream shard schema drift, dedup churn, dataloader behavior
  diverging across backends (workers, prefetch, NUMA)

### 3. ezpz — what the launcher actually does

- `ezpz_setup_env` as the single source of truth for env + PBS/Slurm +
  distributed config
- Backend autodetect: gloo vs xccl vs nccl, gradient placements, mesh
  axis names
- The portability story isn't "we wrote a wrapper" — it's "we caught
  every spot where the assumption was NVIDIA-shaped"

### 4. The fork tax — upstream-sync as a first-class workflow *(differentiator vs other TPC talks)*

- 37+ syncs of `pytorch/torchtitan main` in ~6 weeks
- The **replay pattern**: smoke → resync → reapply our patches on top →
  smoke
- The **smoke discipline**: 50-step burst on `{debugmodel, 2B}` after
  *every* sync; bit-exact loss/grad-norm via `--debug.deterministic
  --debug.seed=42`
- Concrete walk-through: PR #3386 (MoE clean DTensor boundaries) replay
  — `set_moe_sharding_config` + `token_dispatcher.wire_meshes` plumbing,
  what carried, what didn't
- Institutional memory: report-per-run, README indices, dev journal —
  outlives any one developer

### 5. Vendor-portability war stories *(the "bad")*

- **XCCL doesn't implement `barrier()`** → multi-node hangs → fall back
  to gloo for collectives that XCCL doesn't cover
- **DTensor on XPU**: `use_local_output=False` broke attention shapes →
  reverted
- **`DeviceMesh` in saved tensors** (torch 2.13 + AC + TP=2):
  AOT-autograd assertion `expected all tensors_saved_with_vc_check to be
  Tensors`. Fires on every 80B-family config. Smallest repro:
  `agpt_50b_wide` at 2N (~30s). Workaround: compile=OFF or stay on torch
  2.10.
- **Compile is a moving target**: 2–3% noise band across compile
  attempts is the floor for "regression vs noise"
- **Config defaults are NVIDIA-shaped**: vocab projection alone at LBS=8
  OOMs on Intel Max — the config registry's "sensible default" was 31
  GiB requested

### 6. Numerics at scale — the silent killer *(most TPC-relevant)*

- The **TP loss reporting bug**: reported loss = `true_loss /
  dp_world_size` for TP>1, silently, since 2026-04-27. Looked
  "reasonable" on plots. Caught only when a smoke comparison didn't
  match a prior bit-exact baseline.
- Why stdout's 5 sig-figs aren't enough → read `loss` + `grad_norm` from
  TB/W&B directly (cf. `scripts/loss_compare.py`)
- General lesson: **silent correctness bugs are the ones that train
  through to convergence and waste 100k node-hours**

### 7. Optimizer & HP work *(as called out in the abstract)*

- LR-finder framework: AdamW / Muon / SophiaG / Mano × {2B, 20B, 80B}
- Findings worth showing:
  - AdamW most LR-tolerant; SophiaG needs ~10× lower LR
  - **SophiaG and Muon both break at 80B** — algorithmic or numerical?
    open question
  - 80B AdamW landed at LR=1.1e-5 from finder
  - At 2B GBS=384 on 8N, AdamW beat Mano + QKNorm variants
- The tooling itself is portable across configs — LR finder is a
  reusable harness in the config registry

### 8. MoE specifics — what's different and what hurts

- DeepSeek-style MLA + MoE in `experiments/ezpz/moe`
- **EP=2 wiring** through DTensor (PR #3386):
  `wire_meshes(ep_mesh, tp_mesh)` is the new contract
- **MoE + AC + compile = grief**: routing non-determinism →
  `CheckpointError` under AC recomputation → forces AC=none at 7B+
- At 2B scale: EP=2 vs EP=1 is +0.06 GiB / ~1% TPS — token-dispatch
  overhead is essentially free; the picture changes at debugmodel scale
  where all-to-all dominates
- Open: where's the EP↔FSDP frontier at 16B / 64B / 100B?

### 9. Operational reality at 100s of nodes *(the "restarts")*

- Failure taxonomy from production runs:
  - **Transient**: ECC, fabric flaps, single-rank stalls → restart from
    checkpoint
  - **Systemic**: torch-version regression, vendor driver bug →
    diagnose then revert
  - **Silent**: numerics drift, dataloader skew → only caught by smoke
    + deterministic gates
- Async + pinned-memory checkpointing — what works, what's still flaky
- The "separating transient from systemic" cost is mostly human triage,
  not tooling — quantify it (engineer-hours/week)

### 10. What generalizes vs what doesn't *(the "good")*

- **Generalizes**: smoke discipline, deterministic regression gates,
  replay-after-sync, launcher abstraction, journal/report-per-run
  hygiene
- **Doesn't generalize**: compile decisions, AC boundaries, EP
  boundaries, collective tuning
- Surprise: most of "scale" is *process*, not *performance*

### 11. Open questions for TPC *(the ask)*

- A portable bit-exact regression suite across vendors — does anyone
  have one?
- Compile vs eager at 1T scale — defensible decision tree?
- Async-checkpoint Pareto frontier: recovery time × frequency × storage
  cost in production
- Optimizer failure modes at 80B+: algorithmic vs numerical
- MoE EP/FSDP scaling boundaries

## Framing notes

- **Lead with the bug, not the architecture.** The TP loss-reporting bug
  (§6) is a great 60-second opener — it grabs the audience because
  everyone in that room has felt that exact dread.
- **The fork tax (§4) is the moat.** Most TPC talks will be peak-FLOPs
  slides; almost nobody will talk about *the cost of staying on a
  fast-moving upstream*. That's the most differentiated angle.
- **Show one numerical proof slide.** Pick a single smoke comparison
  (e.g. `agpt_2b` byte-identical 24.34 GiB across the 37th sync) —
  concrete bytes are more credible than "we validated it."
- **End with an ask, not a summary.** §11 should be 3 questions to
  actually answer, not a checklist.
